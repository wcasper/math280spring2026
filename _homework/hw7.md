### Directions
Solve the following problems and type up your solutions.  Your solutions should be provided in one of the following formats (in order of preference)
* typed up in $$\LaTeX$$ and submitted as a PDF on Canvas
* written legibly on blank paper, scanned into a PDF and then uploaded on Canvas
* written on ancient parchement with a quill and then flown to the instructor via owl post like in Harry Potter

If you go with the first strategy, you may wish to check out Overleaf which is a free and intuitive website for generating $$\LaTeX$$ documents online.
If you wish to use the second method and don't own a scanner at home, you can check out the numerous scanning apps available for smartphones.

**Problem 1:**

Let $$A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$$ be a nonempty subset which is bounded above and let $$b$$ be an upper bound of $$A$$.
Prove that $$b$$ is the supremum of $$A$$ if and only if for all real numbers $$\epsilon > 0$$ there exists $$a\in A$$ such that  $$b-\epsilon < a$$.

**Problem 2:**

Let $$A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$$.  We say $$m$$ is a **maximal element** of $$A$$ if $$m$$ is an upper bound of $$A$$ and $$m\in A$$.

* (a) Prove that if $$m_1$$ and $$m_2$$ are two maximal elements of $$A$$, then $$m_1=m_2$$.  [Note: this means we can talk about *the* maximal element of $$A$$]
* (b) Prove that a maximum of $$A$$ is also a supremum of $$A$$.
* (c) Give an example showing that a nonempty subset of real numbers which is bounded above doesn't necessarily have a maximal element.

**Problem 3:**

Prove that if $$A$$ is a non-empty, closed subset of $$\mathbb R$$ that is bounded above, then $$A$$ has a maximal element.

**Problem 4:**

Prove that $$\mathbb Z$$ is a closed subset of $$\mathbb R$$.

**Problem 5:**

For each $$i\in\mathbb{N}$$, consider the open intervals $$U_i = (i-1/2,i+1/2)$$ and $$V_i = (0,1/i)$$.

* (a) Explain why $$\{U_i: i\in \mathbb N\}$$ is an open cover of $$\mathbb N$$.  Prove that it has no finite subcover.
* (b) Explain why $$\{U_i: i\in \mathbb N\}$$ is an open cover of $$\{1,2,3\}$$.  Prove that it has a finite subcover.
* (c) Explain why $$\{V_i: i\in \mathbb N\}$$ is an open cover of $$(0,1)$$.  Prove that it has no finite subcover.
* (d) Explain why $$\{V_i: i\in \mathbb N\}$$ is an open cover of $$(0,7/9)$$.  Prove that it has a finite subcover.
